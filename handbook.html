Section 2

General Employment

2.1  At-Will Employment

Employment with Crabb Holdings LLC / HobbyTown Louisville is at-will. This means employees are free to resign at any time, with or without cause, and the Company may terminate the employment relationship at any time, with or without cause or advance notice. As an at-will employee, you are not guaranteed employment for any set period of time. The policies in this handbook are those in effect at the time of publication and may be amended, modified, or terminated at any time, except that the at-will policy itself may be modified only by a signed, written agreement between the CEO and the employee at issue.

2.2  Equal Employment Opportunity

Crabb Holdings LLC / HobbyTown Louisville is an Equal Opportunity Employer. Employment opportunities are based on qualifications and the ability to perform the essential functions of a particular job. The Company makes employment decisions without regard to race, color, religion, sex (including sexual orientation, gender identity, and pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions), national origin, age (40 or older), veteran or military status, disability, genetic information, or any other characteristic protected by applicable federal, state, or local law.

This policy governs all aspects of employment, including recruitment, hiring, job assignment, promotions, transfers, compensation, discipline, termination, layoff, access to benefits and training, and all other terms and privileges of employment.

Coverage note

Several federal anti-discrimination statutes (Title VII, ADA, GINA, PWFA) apply only to employers with 15 or more employees, and ADEA applies only at 20 or more. The Kentucky Civil Rights Act (KCRA) applies to private employers with 8 or more employees, and the Kentucky Pregnant Workers Act (KPWA) applies at 15 or more. The Company commits to the standards in this policy as a matter of internal policy regardless of whether each statute technically applies at our current employee count.

The Company may provide reasonable accommodations as required by law (including for disabilities, sincerely held religious beliefs, pregnancy and related conditions covered in Section 4.6, and lactation), so long as the accommodation does not pose an undue hardship. If you would like to request an accommodation, contact the CEO.

We strongly encourage the reporting of any instances of discrimination or harassment and prohibit retaliation against anyone who makes a good-faith report or participates in an investigation. See Section 6.6 for the complaint procedure, including external reporting options.

2.3  Immigration Law Compliance

Crabb Holdings LLC / HobbyTown Louisville is committed to employing only individuals authorized to work in the United States. In compliance with the Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986, as amended, each new employee must complete the Employment Eligibility Verification Form I-9 and present documents establishing identity and employment eligibility on or before their first day of work. The Company uses the most current edition of Form I-9 issued by USCIS. Former employees who are rehired must also complete a new Form I-9 if their previous I-9 is more than three years old or is no longer retained or valid.

2.4  Employee Grievances

We want a harmonious workplace, and we want to know when something isn't working. Raise concerns about working conditions, communication, or interpersonal conflict with your supervisor first. If a concern is not resolved at that level, or if it would be inappropriate to discuss with your supervisor, submit a signed written grievance to the CEO. The Company may hold a meeting with the employee, supervisor, and any other individuals who can help resolve the issue. Discussions will be limited to those involved with resolving it.

Complaints involving alleged discrimination or harassment shall be processed under the Anti-Harassment policy in Section 6.6. The Company assures that all employees filing a grievance can do so without fear of retaliation.

2.5  Internal Communication

Effective communication is essential. The Company maintains bulletin boards, group chats, and email for sharing important information. All employees are responsible for checking internal communications regularly. To avoid confusion, employees should not post or remove material from the bulletin boards without manager approval.

2.6  Outside Employment

Employees may hold outside jobs as long as they continue to meet the performance standards of their position with HobbyTown Louisville. Unless an alternative work schedule has been approved in writing, you remain subject to the Company's scheduling demands, including availability for overtime when necessary. Company property, office space, equipment, materials, trade secrets, and any confidential information may not be used for outside employment.

2.7  Anti-Retaliation & Whistleblower Policy

The Company prohibits retaliation against any employee who, in good faith:

  • Makes or threatens to make a complaint about a suspected violation of law, including discriminatory or unfair employment practices.
  • Reports concerns about accounting, internal accounting controls, or auditing matters.
  • Reports a violation that endangers the health or safety of an employee, customer, or the public.

Anyone found to have engaged in retaliation, or in violation of any law or policy, will be subject to discipline up to and including termination. Employees who knowingly make a false report will also be subject to discipline.

To report a violation, contact your supervisor or Bryce Crabb directly at brycecrabb@gmail.com or (501) 628-7650.

2.8  Background Checks

Where permitted by law, the Company may conduct background checks on applicants and employees, which may include criminal-history checks, reference checks, and verification of credentials. The Company evaluates criminal-history information on a case-by-case basis, considering the nature and gravity of the offense, the time elapsed since the offense, and its relevance to the position sought.

Section 3

Employment Status & Recordkeeping

3.1  Employment Classifications

For purposes of salary administration and eligibility for overtime and benefits, employees are classified as either exempt or non-exempt under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) and Kentucky law. Non-exempt employees are entitled to overtime pay in accordance with federal and state law (see Section 7.6). Exempt employees are paid a fixed salary that meets the applicable salary threshold and perform duties that satisfy the executive, administrative, professional, or other recognized exemption tests. As of the effective date of this handbook, the federal salary threshold for the executive, administrative, and professional exemptions is $684 per week ($35,568 annually); Kentucky follows the federal threshold.

In addition to your exempt or non-exempt designation, you also belong to one of the following categories:

  • Full-time: Regularly scheduled to work 30 hours or more per week. Generally eligible for Company benefits, subject to plan terms.
  • Part-time: Regularly scheduled to work fewer than 30 hours per week. May be eligible for some benefits, subject to plan terms.
  • Temporary/Seasonal: Hired for a limited time or specific project (e.g., holiday season). Generally not entitled to Company benefits beyond statutory benefits required by law.

If your position or responsibilities change, the CEO will inform you of any change in your classification or exempt status.

3.2  Personnel Data Changes

It is your responsibility to promptly notify your supervisor or the CEO of any changes to your personnel data. Such changes can affect your eligibility for benefits, premium amounts, and your receipt of important Company information. Please notify us as soon as possible if any of the following changes: legal name; mailing address; telephone number(s); beneficiary designations; tax-form withholdings; emergency contact(s); training certificates or professional licenses.

3.3  Expense Reimbursement

The Company reimburses employees for necessary, reasonable, and pre-approved expenses incurred in the course of doing their jobs. Get advance approval from the CEO before incurring an expense. Reimbursable items may include mileage, ground or air transportation, lodging, business meals, and other expenses required by law. Submit expense reports to the CEO with receipts or other documentation. Make a reasonable effort to limit business expenses to economical options.

3.4  Termination of Employment

Termination of employment is an inevitable part of any organization. The following procedures apply.

Notice of Voluntary Separation

Employees who intend to leave the Company are asked to provide at least two weeks' written notice. This allows the Company time to adjust without placing an undue burden on coworkers.

Return of Company Property

On or before your last day, return all keys, files, records, uniforms, and any other Company property in your possession.

Final Pay

Under Kentucky law (KRS 337.055), the Company will pay your final wages no later than the next regular payday or 14 days after the date of separation, whichever is later, regardless of whether the separation was voluntary or involuntary. Final pay includes all earned wages through your last day worked, plus any earned commissions or bonuses payable under the Company's established plans or policies. Accrued PTO is paid out in accordance with Section 5.2.

Benefits Upon Termination

All accrued and vested benefits that are due and payable at termination will be paid in accordance with applicable federal, state, and local laws. Federal COBRA continuation coverage applies only to employers with 20 or more employees and therefore does not apply to the Company at our current size. Kentucky's mini-continuation statute (KRS 304.18-110) may permit continuation of fully insured group health coverage for up to 18 months at the former employee's expense. The Company will provide you with the carrier's continuation paperwork and contact information at separation; specific terms, deadlines, and rates are set by the carrier and the plan documents.

3.5  Minor Employment & Child Labor (Kentucky)

The Company complies with all federal and Kentucky child-labor laws, including the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) and KRS 339. Employees under age 18 are subject to additional restrictions on hours, scheduling, and the types of work they may perform. The Company will not knowingly employ anyone under age 16. For employees ages 16 and 17, the following limits apply:

During school weeks

  • No more than 6 hours per school day; no more than 8 hours on a non-school day.
  • No more than 30 hours per school week; no more than 40 hours per non-school week.
  • No work between 10:30 PM and 6:00 AM before a school day. With written parental and school permission, work may extend until 1:00 AM on a non-school night.

During non-school weeks (summer and breaks)

  • Up to 8 hours per day and 40 hours per week.
  • No work between 1:00 AM and 6:00 AM.

Hazardous-occupation restrictions

Employees under 18 are prohibited from performing hazardous occupations as defined by KRS 339.230 and 29 CFR Part 570, including (without limitation) operating power-driven hoisting or paper-product machinery, working from heights that require fall protection, and using certain power tools. At HobbyTown Louisville, minors may not operate the box compactor (if any), use power saws or grinders, or work alone after dark.

Before a minor employee may begin work, the Company requires proof of age and any working papers or school documentation required by Kentucky law. Schedules for minor employees must be reviewed and approved by the Manager. Speak with the CEO with any questions about hours, duties, or breaks for minor employees.

Severe weather, fires, power failures, and similar emergencies can disrupt operations. The decision to close or delay regular operations rests with the CEO. When a decision is made, you will be notified directly. In a declared state of emergency that prohibits travel, do not report to work.

Pay during a closure:

4.3  Workplace Safety

We are committed to providing a clean, safe, and healthful work environment. The Company and all employees must comply with all occupational safety and health standards established by OSHA and Kentucky OSH (KOSHA), as well as state and local regulations. Obey safety rules and exercise common sense in all work activities.

Report any unsafe conditions to your supervisor immediately, without fear of reprisal. Report any work-related injury, no matter how minor, to your supervisor as soon as possible so we can document it and provide care. If reporting to your supervisor is not appropriate, contact Bryce Crabb directly at brycecrabb@gmail.com or (501) 628-7650.

4.5  Meal & Break Periods

The Company complies with Kentucky's meal and rest period requirements. For non-exempt employees:

  • Meal period: A reasonable meal period scheduled as close as possible to the middle of the shift, in accordance with KRS 337.355. The Company will not require an employee to take a meal period sooner than 3 hours, or later than 5 hours, after the start of the shift. Meal periods of 30 minutes or more are unpaid; bona fide breaks of less than 30 minutes are paid.
  • Rest period: A reasonable paid rest period during each 4-hour work period, in accordance with KRS 337.365 and 803 KAR 1:065. The Company generally provides a 10-minute paid rest period in the middle of each 4-hour work period; this is the Company's standard practice and may be adjusted to accommodate operating needs.

Non-exempt employees must be fully relieved of duty during unpaid meal periods and must record the start and end of each meal period using the Company's timekeeping system. If you do not take a meal or rest period to which you are entitled, notify your supervisor immediately. Supervisors schedule meal and rest periods to accommodate operating needs.

4.6  Pregnancy & Lactation Accommodations

Under the federal Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (PWFA) and the Kentucky Pregnant Workers Act (KPWA), the Company will provide reasonable accommodations to qualified employees and applicants with known limitations related to pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions, unless the accommodation would impose an undue hardship. Examples of accommodations may include:

  • More frequent or longer breaks (including for water, food, restroom, or rest).
  • Modified work schedules or job restructuring.
  • A temporary transfer to a less strenuous or less hazardous position, or light duty.
  • Acquisition or modification of equipment, including appropriate seating.
  • Time off to attend appointments or to recover from childbirth or a related condition.
  • A private space (other than a bathroom) for expressing breast milk.

If you need an accommodation, notify your supervisor or the CEO. We will engage in a timely, good-faith, interactive process to identify an effective accommodation. The Company will not require you to take leave if another reasonable accommodation will allow you to continue working, and we will not retaliate against you for requesting an accommodation.

Lactation breaks (PUMP Act)

Under the federal PUMP for Nursing Mothers Act, the Company provides reasonable break time and a private space (other than a bathroom) shielded from view and free from intrusion for nursing employees to express breast milk for up to one year following the birth of the employee's child. Frequency, duration, and timing of pumping breaks will vary based on each employee's individual needs.

When an employee uses break time to express breast milk and the break overlaps with a paid rest break, that time is paid. If an employee takes additional pumping time outside of paid breaks, that time is unpaid unless the employee is not completely relieved of duty.

How to request an accommodation

Talk to your supervisor or contact the CEO directly. You do not need a doctor's note to start the conversation. We may ask for documentation only when reasonable under the circumstances. Requests are kept confidential to the extent possible.

5.2  Paid Time Off (PTO)

PTO is an all-purpose time-off bank for vacation, illness, injury, or personal business. PTO is paid at your base rate at the time of the absence and does not include overtime, bonuses, commissions, or shift differentials. Eligibility and accrual:

  • Eligibility: Full-time employees who have completed their introductory period (90 days).
  • Annual amount: 10 PTO days per calendar year (80 hours).
  • Pro-ration: Employees hired after January 1 receive a pro-rated allotment for their first calendar year.
  • Carryover: Up to 40 hours (5 days) of unused PTO may be carried over to the following calendar year. PTO above that amount that is unused as of December 31 is forfeited unless carryover is required by law.
  • Payout at separation: Accrued and unused PTO is not paid out at separation, whether the separation is voluntary or involuntary. Kentucky law does not require employers to pay out accrued PTO at separation, and the Company has elected not to do so.

Request PTO in writing at least 2 weeks in advance whenever possible. For unexpected needs (sudden illness or emergency), notify your direct supervisor as early as possible, and again on each additional day of absence. Work-related injuries or illnesses are covered under Workers' Compensation (Section 5.5) and are not deducted from PTO.

5.3  Health Insurance

Eligible full-time employees may elect to participate in the Company's health insurance plan. Coverage details, costs, eligibility waiting periods, and dependent coverage are described in the Summary Plan Description (SPD), which controls in the event of any conflict with this handbook. Contact the CEO for plan documents and current rates.

5.4  Employee Discount Program

Employees receive a discount on HobbyTown Louisville products equivalent to the Company's cost plus 10%. The discount is for personal use by the employee only; it may not be used to purchase items on behalf of friends, family, or third parties for resale. Any prohibited use may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination, and the loss of the discount privilege. Special-order items, consigned goods, and certain manufacturer-restricted items may be excluded from the discount; see the CEO with questions.

5.5  Workers' Compensation

Employees who are injured on the job are eligible for Kentucky workers' compensation benefits, which cover medical treatment and a portion of lost wages for any injury or illness sustained in the course of employment. Coverage is paid for in full by the Company; no premium is charged to employees and no individual enrollment is required.

Report all job-related accidents or illnesses to your supervisor immediately. Your supervisor will contact the CEO to obtain claim forms and provide instructions. Failure to promptly report an injury can affect coverage and benefits.

5.6  Military Leave

The Company grants unpaid time off for service, training, and other obligations in the uniformed services in accordance with the Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act (USERRA) and applicable Kentucky law. Provide advance notice to your supervisor unless military necessity prevents it. Health-insurance continuation is available during military leave subject to plan terms and applicable law. Reemployment rights, pay-level restoration, and benefits-service credit are governed by USERRA. Contact the CEO for reinstatement procedures.

5.7  Jury Duty

We encourage employees to fulfill their civic responsibility when called to serve as a juror. Provide your supervisor with a copy of your jury summons as soon as possible. Report to work on workdays (or parts of workdays) when you are not required to serve. Jury duty leave is unpaid for non-exempt employees, except as required by Kentucky law; you may use accrued PTO. Exempt employees who miss work because of jury duty receive their full salary for any week in which they perform any work, less any jury fees received, to the extent permitted by law. Kentucky law prohibits retaliation against employees for serving on a jury.

5.8  Voting Leave

Whenever possible, employees should vote before or after work hours. Under Kentucky law (KRS 118.035), employees are entitled to a reasonable period (not less than 4 hours) of leave to vote or to apply for an absentee ballot, provided the request is made before the day of the election. The Company may specify the hours during which an employee may take voting leave. Voting leave is unpaid; employees may use accrued PTO.

5.9  Election Officer Leave

Employees selected to serve as precinct election officers are granted leave to attend training or to serve as an election officer. The Company may specify the hours during which leave may be taken. The leave is unpaid; employees may use accrued PTO. Provide reasonable advance notice and certification to verify your eligibility.

5.10  Emergency Response Leave

Employees who are volunteer firefighters, rescue squad members, EMTs, peace officers, or members of an emergency management agency may be allowed an unpaid leave of absence to respond to an emergency that occurs prior to the start of their shift. Make every effort to notify your supervisor before your shift begins. To verify eligibility, provide certification from the supervisor of the volunteer agency, including the date and time of the emergency response. You may use accrued PTO for this leave.

5.11  Adoptive Parent Leave

Under Kentucky law (KRS 337.015), employees who adopt a child under the age of 7 are entitled to a reasonable period of leave to bond with the adopted child, on the same terms as leave provided to a birth parent. Provide written notice as soon as the need for leave is known. If the Company provides paid leave to birth parents for bonding, leave under this section is paid; otherwise, employees may use accrued PTO. The Company will not retaliate against any employee for exercising rights under this law.

5.12  Bereavement Leave

Eligible full-time employees may take up to three (3) consecutive workdays of paid bereavement leave following the death of an immediate family member, defined as: spouse, domestic partner, parent, child, sibling, grandparent, grandchild, parent-in-law, sibling-in-law, child-in-law, or any person who shared the employee's household. Up to one (1) workday of paid bereavement leave is available for the death of an extended family member (aunt, uncle, niece, nephew, first cousin).

Part-time and seasonal employees may take unpaid bereavement leave on the same terms. Employees may use accrued PTO to extend bereavement leave. Additional unpaid time off may be approved at the CEO's discretion based on the circumstances. Notify your supervisor as soon as possible and, if requested, provide reasonable documentation (e.g., obituary, funeral program).

Our rules and standards of conduct are essential to a productive workplace. The list below is not exhaustive; other forms of misconduct may also result in discipline up to and including termination:

  • Theft or inappropriate removal or possession of property.
  • Falsification of timekeeping records.
  • Possession, distribution, sale, transfer, manufacture, or use of alcohol or illegal drugs in the workplace (see Section 6.5).

  • Smoking in the workplace.
  • Harassment of any kind, including sexual or other unlawful harassment (see Section 6.6).
  • Excessive absenteeism or tardiness.
  • Unauthorized use of telephones, computers, or other company-owned equipment on working time.
  • Unauthorized disclosure of business secrets or other confidential or non-public proprietary information. (Wages and other terms and conditions of employment are not considered confidential information.)

Nothing in this policy is intended to restrict an employee's right to discuss, or act together to improve, wages, benefits, and working conditions with coworkers, or in any way restrict employees' rights under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA).

6.2  Disciplinary Action

Discipline is intended to fairly correct behavior or performance issues early. Steps may include verbal warning, written warning, suspension with or without pay, and termination, depending on the severity of the problem and frequency of occurrence. The Company reserves the right to administer discipline at its discretion based on the circumstances. Some violations are serious enough to justify immediate termination without prior steps, including but not limited to workplace violence, harassment, theft of any kind, gross insubordination, vandalism or destruction of company property, presence on company property during non-business hours without authorization, unauthorized use of company equipment or vehicles, or material misrepresentation to a customer, prospective customer, the public, or an employee.

6.3  Confidentiality

The Company takes the protection of Confidential Information seriously. Confidential Information includes (but is not limited to) computer processes and codes, customer lists and preferences, customers' personal information, financial data, marketing strategies, proprietary production processes, pricing information, business and marketing plans, vendor information, software, databases, and information concerning the creation, acquisition, or disposition of products and services. It also includes intellectual property such as trade secrets, ideas, discoveries, writings, trademarks, and inventions developed in the course of your employment. Wages and other conditions of employment are not Confidential Information.

Do not disclose Confidential Information to any unauthorized individual, during or after your employment. If you receive a request for Confidential Information, refer it to your supervisor.

Defend Trade Secrets Act notice (18 U.S.C. § 1833(b))

Federal law provides that an individual will not be held criminally or civilly liable under any federal or state trade-secret law for the disclosure of a trade secret that (a) is made in confidence to a federal, state, or local government official, either directly or indirectly, or to an attorney, and is made solely for the purpose of reporting or investigating a suspected violation of law; or (b) is made in a complaint or other document filed in a lawsuit or other proceeding, if filed under seal. An individual who files a lawsuit alleging retaliation for reporting a suspected violation of law may disclose the trade secret to that individual's attorney and use the trade-secret information in the court proceeding, provided the individual files any document containing the trade secret under seal and does not disclose the trade secret except pursuant to court order.

6.4  Workplace Violence

The Company strictly prohibits workplace violence, including any act of intimidation, threat, harassment, physical violence, verbal abuse, aggression, or coercion against a coworker, vendor, customer, or visitor. Prohibited actions include:

  • Physically injuring another person.
  • Threatening to injure another person.
  • Engaging in behavior that subjects another person to emotional distress.
  • Using obscene, abusive, or threatening language or gestures.
  • Bringing any firearm or other weapon into the store, the back room, or any other interior area of company property. Concealed-carry license holders are not exempt from this prohibition while inside the building.
  • Threatening to use or using a weapon while on company premises, on company-related business, or during job-related functions.
  • Intentionally damaging property.

Firearms in personal vehicles (KRS 237.106)

Kentucky law permits employees and other persons holding a license or permit to carry a firearm to keep firearms or ammunition in their personal vehicle while parked on the Company's property, provided the firearm is stored out of sight. The Company will not interfere with this right and will not search employee vehicles for firearms. The interior-prohibition above applies only inside the building.

Report all threats or acts of violence immediately to your supervisor or the CEO. Reports will be investigated promptly and kept confidential to the maximum extent possible. Retaliation against anyone who makes a good-faith report is strictly prohibited.

6.5  Drug & Alcohol Use

The Company is committed to maintaining a workplace free of substance abuse. Section 8

Daily Shift Duties

Below are your day-to-day responsibilities at HobbyTown Louisville. The store's reputation is built on a clean space, well-merchandised product, and helpful staff. These duties make that happen every day.

Aligned to corporate standards

The shift-duty structure below mirrors the HobbyTown corporate Operations Manual (Workday section), adapted for our Louisville store. For full procedural detail (e.g., SmartWeb receiving, cash-handling forms, inventory counts, customer-experience methodology), see the store's internal Operations & Training Guide, which supplements, but does not replace, the corporate Operations Manual.

8.1  Opening Checklist

Opening procedures must be completed every day. The Manager on Duty owns the open and works the manager list; team members work the team list.

Arrival: Manager on Duty arrives 30 minutes before doors open. Team Members arrive 10 minutes before opening (30 minutes during the holiday season).

Manager-only tasks

  • Pre-shift: clock in, review yesterday's punches, fix any missed punches.
  • Review yesterday's sales, margin focus, and today's sales target.
  • Check store email and corporate communications.
  • Opening drawers counted ($100 per drawer); coin change verified; counted in front of manager.
  • POS, credit card terminal, receipt printer, and internet all tested.
  • Remind the team of products to highlight, new releases, newly received product, and priorities for the day.
  • Doors unlocked on time; greet every customer within 10 ft / 3 sec.

Team tasks

  • Sidewalk and storefront exterior clean and debris-free.
  • Front signage clean and visible; doors and windows free of fingerprints.
  • Floors, counters, registers, and high-touch surfaces clean.
  • Restrooms fresh and fully supplied (TP, napkins, hand soap).
  • Aisles walked, with every product front-faced and price tags legible (including impulse displays).
  • Endcaps and feature areas merchandised to drive sales and margin.
  • Open-sign light, Gundam sign light, display-case lights, TVs, and music all on.

8.2  Mid-Shift / During the Day

Customers always come first. When you're not actively assisting a customer, work the lists below. The corporate Customer Experience playbook (3-Second Greeting, Discovery Process, L.A.R.A. complaint resolution, professional phone etiquette) governs every customer interaction.

Manager-only tasks

  • Coach team members on the greeting standard and customer engagement throughout the shift.
  • Check email and corporate communications at least once mid-shift.
  • Review and resolve any escalated customer complaints.
  • Walk the floor to inspect merchandising standards and reset anything out of compliance.
  • Review the negative in-stock report in SmartWeb and correct any discrepancies.

Team tasks

  • Greet every customer within 10 ft / 3 sec; thank them and invite them back.
  • Ask for phone number and email address for the Hobby+ program during every transaction. They will receive exclusive discounts.
  • Suggestive-sell add-ons (paint, glue, batteries, chargers) on relevant sales.
  • Receive all incoming shipments: verify packing slips, log discrepancies, ensure cost on the invoice matches SmartWeb, label products appropriately, post to new arrivals, and merchandise in the store appropriately.
  • Process special orders and layaways: call customers with outstanding pickups.
  • 1:00 PM aisle walk: front-face product, fix crooked or missing price tags, return stray items to their home, organize impulse displays.
  • 4:00 PM aisle walk: front-face product, fix crooked or missing price tags, return stray items to their home, organize impulse displays.
  • 7:00 PM aisle walk: front-face product, fix crooked or missing price tags, return stray items to their home, organize impulse displays.
  • Wipe down front counter, RC counter, and display cases as needed.
  • Fill any holes on shelves and displays from backstock.
  • Check restrooms mid-shift; top up napkins, TP, and hand soap.
  • Flag inventory holes or low-stock items to a manager for reorder.

8.3  Closing Checklist

Closing procedures must be completed every day. The Manager on Duty owns the close and works the manager list; team members work the team list.

Manager-only tasks

  • Continue upselling and add-on selling through close; don't signal closing early.
  • Close registers per the cash-handling policy; count all drawers.
  • Investigate and document any cash variances of $2 or more; recount; contact Bryce if persistent.
  • Prepare and seal the bank deposit for tomorrow's drop.
  • Review sales and profit vs. goals to identify trends.
  • Document key wins, misses, customer feedback, and follow-ups.
  • Confirm all customers have exited; check restrooms and back area.
  • Turn off all store and display lights, TVs, music, and non-essential equipment.
  • Lock all doors before leaving.

Team tasks

  • Maintain full customer engagement until the last customer leaves.
  • Recover aisles; face gondolas, shelves, and endcaps; line up models neatly.
  • Vacuum floors and mop as needed.
  • Clean doors, windows, glass, counters, and high-touch surfaces.
  • Empty all trash and remove from the store.
  • Clean restrooms; restock toilet paper, napkins, and hand soap.
  • Confirm with the manager that the cash count and bank deposit are handled before leaving.

Cash drawer standards (corporate)

  • One drawer per cashier per shift. Drawers are never shared. No personal money in the drawer.
  • Each drawer opens with $100. Cashier counts in front of a manager; manager confirms total and adequate change.
  • All $50 and $100 bills must be checked for authenticity using approved verification methods, then stored under the cash drawer.
  • Suspected counterfeit bills must not be accepted and must be reported.

Safety first

Never make the bank deposit alone after dark if you feel unsafe; call Bryce. The deposit can wait until the next morning if needed.

Section 9

Job Description: Store Team Member

About this job description

This job description is aligned to HobbyTown's corporate "Staff Job Description – Executive Standard" (April 2026), with HobbyTown Louisville-specific additions for reporting relationship, physical requirements, work environment, and ADA-compliant essential functions. It describes the general nature and level of work performed and is not an exhaustive list of all duties, responsibilities, or qualifications. Duties may be added, removed, or changed by the Company at any time, with or without notice.

9.1  Role Purpose

The HobbyTown Store Team Member plays a critical role in driving sales, improving margin, and delivering a premium customer experience. The Team Member is responsible for actively engaging customers, helping them find the right solutions, supporting merchandising standards, and contributing to overall store performance and profitability. Team Members are expected to represent the HobbyTown brand with professionalism, energy, and a strong customer-first mindset.

At HobbyTown Louisville specifically, you'll work across our core hobby categories (toys, games, radio control, models, and more), process transactions on the SmartSuite POS, maintain a clean and well-merchandised sales floor, and support back-of-house functions like receiving, pricing, and inventory.

Position type: Non-exempt, hourly. Full-time or part-time.
Compensation: Hourly rate based on experience, plus employee discount (Section 5.4).

9.2  Reporting Relationship

Reports to the Store Manager and ultimately to the CEO. Works collaboratively with other Store Team Members and may receive day-to-day direction from the Shift Lead or Manager on Duty.

9.3  Essential Duties & Responsibilities

Customer service & sales

  • Greet every customer warmly within 10 feet or 3 seconds of entry (corporate experience standard); build rapport and identify the kind of help they need.
  • Listen to customers and ask thoughtful questions to understand their hobby, skill level, and goals; recommend appropriate products, kits, paints, batteries, motors, and accessories.
  • Demonstrate products on the sales floor (e.g., RC vehicles, drones, model kits) and answer technical questions accurately. Bring difficult questions to a Shift Lead or the Manager.
  • Suggest add-on items (paints, glue, brushes, batteries, chargers, etc.) that complete the sale and set the customer up for success at home.
  • Resolve customer concerns professionally; escalate refunds, returns, exchanges, and disputes to the Manager when they exceed your authority.
  • Process special orders and layaways accurately; follow up with customers on outstanding orders per Section 8.2.

Cash handling & POS

  • Operate the point-of-sale (SmartSuite) system to ring up sales, process credit/debit/cash transactions, returns, exchanges, gift cards, and store credit.
  • Verify pricing on items at checkout and apply discounts and employee discounts correctly.
  • Count your assigned cash drawer at the start and end of each shift; report any discrepancies of more than $2 immediately to the Shift Lead, recount, and contact the CEO if the discrepancy persists.
  • Prepare bank deposits at the end of the day per Section 8.3.

Store operations & merchandising

  • Complete opening, midday, and closing duties as defined in Section 8.
  • Receive incoming shipments: verify packing slips against contents, log discrepancies, price items per Company guidelines, and stock them on the floor.
  • Run price updates and apply price-tag stickers to products when prices change.
  • Maintain front-facing, fully stocked shelves and clean, organized display cases throughout the day. Fill holes from backstock as soon as they appear.
  • Build and maintain attractive product displays (e.g., seasonal endcaps and feature areas).
  • Conduct cycle counts and assist with periodic full inventory.
  • Identify slow-moving or discontinued products and inventory "holes" and report to the Manager.
  • Keep the sales floor, restrooms, counters, and back room clean and presentable.

Product knowledge & continuous learning

  • Develop and maintain working knowledge of our core categories: toys, games, radio control (cars, trucks, boats, planes, drones), model kits (cars, aircraft, ships, armor), rockets (Estes/AeroTech), paints and finishing supplies, and hobby tools.
  • Stay current on new releases, manufacturer promotions, and seasonal trends.
  • Participate in vendor trainings and Company-provided product education.
  • Share product knowledge with teammates so the whole team can serve customers better.

Safety, security & compliance

  • Follow all Company safety, security, and conduct policies in this handbook.
  • Be alert for shoplifting and report suspicious activity to a Shift Lead or Manager. Never physically confront a suspected shoplifter.
  • Use the alarm system, lock procedures, and key handling protocols correctly per Sections 4.4 and 8.
  • Complete required training (e.g., harassment prevention, safety) on time.

9.4  Required Qualifications

  • Must be at least 16 years of age (and meet all Kentucky child-labor restrictions for minors).
  • High school diploma or equivalent, or actively enrolled in high school.
  • Authorized to work in the United States; able to complete Form I-9.
  • Reliable, punctual, and able to work a flexible retail schedule, including weekends and extended hours during the holiday season.
  • Strong interpersonal and communication skills; able to interact courteously with a wide range of customers, including beginners and experienced hobbyists.
  • Basic math skills sufficient to handle cash transactions and verify drawer counts.
  • Comfortable with point-of-sale systems and willing to learn SmartSuite.
  • Honest, dependable, and able to handle cash and inventory with integrity.
  • Able to follow Company policies and instructions from the Manager and Shift Leads.

9.5  Preferred Qualifications

  • Personal experience or strong interest in one or more of our hobby categories (toys, games, RC, models, rockets, etc.).
  • Prior retail, food-service, or other customer-facing work experience.
  • Prior experience with cash handling or POS systems.
  • Experience building, painting, or repairing models, RC vehicles, or similar.
  • Bilingual (Spanish/English) is a plus.

9.6  Physical Requirements

The physical demands described here are representative of those that must be met by a team member to successfully perform the essential functions of this job. Reasonable accommodations may be made to enable individuals with disabilities to perform the essential functions, in accordance with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and Kentucky law.

  • Stand and walk for the majority of an 8-hour shift.
  • Frequently bend, stoop, kneel, reach overhead, and twist to stock and retrieve product.
  • Lift and carry up to 40 pounds regularly (e.g., cases of paint, RC vehicles, model boxes), and occasionally up to 50 pounds with assistance.
  • Climb a step stool or short ladder to access top shelves and stockroom shelving.
  • Use hands and fingers to operate the POS, count cash, demonstrate small parts, and handle fragile merchandise.
  • Vision sufficient to read price tags, packaging, computer screens, and identify small parts.
  • Hearing sufficient to communicate with customers in person and by telephone.

9.7  Work Environment & Schedule

  • Indoor retail environment with moderate noise (customer conversation, occasional RC vehicle demonstrations, music).
  • Occasional exposure to dust, small-parts dust, and the smell of paints, glues, and lubricants (typical of a hobby shop).

  • Holiday season (October through December) typically requires increased availability and longer shifts.
  • Occasional offsite events such as trade shows, demonstrations, or community events may be available.

  • Sales productivity and average transaction value.
  • Accuracy of cash handling, POS transactions, and end-of-day reconciliations.
  • Adherence to opening, midday, and closing duties (Section 8) and to all policies in this handbook.
  • Reliability: attendance, punctuality, and the ability to be counted on by teammates.
  • Product knowledge growth and willingness to learn.
  • Cleanliness and merchandising of the store during your shift.
  • Teamwork: supporting coworkers and contributing to a positive workplace.

Performance is reviewed informally on an ongoing basis and formally at least once per year. Pay adjustments and advancement opportunities are based on performance, business needs, and the Company's discretion.
